# Credit System — Requirements

This document is the testable specification for the credit
subsystem — the accounts a member holds, the credits they contain,
and the rules by which enrolling in and attending a **programme**
consumes them.

It is written as plain-English use cases. It deliberately avoids
naming specific HTTP endpoints or source files so that the underlying
API surface can evolve (compact, split, rename) without invalidating
the requirements. Each rule describes *what* the system must allow or
forbid, not *how* a client reaches it.

Credit is **not** event-agnostic. It applies to programmes only.
Camps and one-off events are prepaid or free and never touch this
subsystem (R6). Attendance mechanics live in
`docs/attendance_requirements.md` and enrollment state transitions in
`docs/enrollment_requirements.md`; both are referenced from here
rather than restated.

**The subsystem is implemented (#294).** The code defines current
behaviour; this document records it. Each rule's test names it with
`@pytest.mark.requirement("credit:Rn")`, and
`tests/test_requirement_coverage.py` checks every marked rule has one.
Credit is an optional module: its tests switch it on per test (R92).

Convention:

- **[✅]** — positive ability is wired and covered by tests.
- **[X]** — prohibition is enforced and covered by tests.
- **[GAP]** — agreed, not yet implemented; cites the open issue that
  introduces it.
- **[UNTESTED]** — implemented, but no test proves it yet.

## Vocabulary

- A **credit account** is a named pot of credits belonging to exactly
  one member. It has an id, a balance, a validity window, and a
  binding (R2). Accounts are the unit of ownership; a member does not
  have "a balance", they have accounts.
- The **account id** is an 8-character letter-and-digit code, shown to
  both the member and the admin, and used to refer to the account in
  every conversation about it.
- An **event-bound** account is usable only for one programme. A
  **general** account is usable for any programme.
- The **validity window** is the period during which an account's
  credits may be spent. It applies to event-bound and general accounts
  alike.
- A **usable** account is one that is open, whose validity window
  contains **the current instant**, and whose balance is greater than
  zero.
- An account's **state** is derived, never stored: `closed` once a
  transfer has drained it, else `expired` outside its window, else
  `empty` at a zero balance, else `usable`.
- A programme keeps **one event id for life**. Extending, terminating
  or splitting it changes its schedule, not its id
  (`docs/event_schedule_model.md`), so the id is the only identity an
  event-bound account or a charge needs. (An earlier model gave each
  split a new id and made credit follow a "chain root"; that model is
  gone.)
- The **occurrence cost** is the number of credits one occurrence of a
  programme consumes.
- A **ledger entry** records one movement of credits into or out of one
  account, with its reason and its actor. Entries are never modified
  or deleted (R80).
- **Staff** = users with the `admin` or `coach` role, or super-admin.
- **Organizer** = the user named as the event's organizer.

## Account shape and invariants

- **R1** [✅] An account belongs to exactly one member. There
  is no shared, family, or guardian-held account (R78).
- **R2** [✅] An account is either **event-bound** (carries a
  programme's id) or **general** (carries none). There is no
  third kind and no account that is both.
- **R3** [✅] Every account carries a validity window, whether
  event-bound or general. There is no perpetual account: opening one
  without a window is rejected.
- **R4** [✅] An account's balance is the sum of its ledger
  entries. There is no stored balance column to fall out of step with
  the entries.
- **R5** [✅] An account id is an 8-character code drawn from
  letters and digits, unique across all accounts, generated by the
  server, and never reused or reassigned.
- **R6** [X] Only programmes consume credit. An account can
  never be bound to a camp or a one-off event (→ 422
  `CREDIT_NOT_APPLICABLE`), and no camp or one-off operation ever reads,
  checks, or spends credit.
- **R7** [✅] An event-bound account stores the programme's id.
  The id never changes, so extending, terminating or splitting a
  programme never strands its credits: a split programme's bound
  accounts keep paying for it.
- **R8** [✅] An account is bound by naming the programme it is for,
  and that id is what the account carries (R7). There is no chain to
  resolve and nothing for the client to know beyond the programme.
- **R9** [✅] A member may hold any number of accounts,
  including several event-bound accounts for the same programme, and
  several general accounts.
- **R10** [✅] There is no default account, and no member or
  admin setting that nominates one. Which account pays is decided by
  the selection order (R25–R29) every time, from the accounts' own
  state.
- **R11** [✅] An account with a zero balance, or one past its
  validity window, is retained indefinitely. Accounts are never
  deleted or purged.
- **R12** [X] A balance can never be negative. Any operation
  that would take it below zero is rejected before it is applied.

## Authorization

- **R13** [X] Opening an account requires the `admin` role.
  A coach cannot open one, and neither can the organizer of the
  programme by virtue of being the organizer.
- **R14** [X] Adjusting an account — extending validity,
  reversing a grant, transferring on departure, applying a penalty —
  requires the `admin` role.
- **R15** [X] A member may read their own accounts, balances,
  validity, and statement. They may not open, adjust, or close one, and
  they may not read another member's.
- **R16** [✅] Staff may read any member's accounts and
  statement.
- **R17** [X] An anonymous caller cannot reach any credit
  operation → 401.

## Opening accounts

- **R18** [✅] An admin opens an account by naming the member,
  the credit amount, the validity window, and either the programme
  (event-bound) or nothing (general).
- **R18a** [✅ #451] An account may be opened for any existing user
  but the super admin (R18b), whatever their account status:
  `registered`, `pending`, `active`, `blocked` or `left`. Status is not
  a credit gate; enrollment and attendance keep their own gates. A
  payment can so be recorded while a new member's registration is still
  under review. An unknown user is refused.
- **R18b** [X] The super admin cannot hold credit: opening an account
  for them, whoever asks → 422 `SUPER_ADMIN_CANNOT_HOLD_CREDIT`, and no
  account is opened (#519).
- **R19** [X] The opening amount must be a positive whole
  number. Zero-credit and fractional accounts are rejected.
- **R20** [X] An existing account is **never topped up**.
  There is no recharge, renewal, or extend-balance operation. More
  credits means a new account — a package bought, not a balance
  refilled.
- **R21** [✅] One movement adds credit to an existing account, and
  only this one: a refund arising from an attendance reversal (R54–R57).
  It corrects a movement that already happened; it is not a new
  purchase. A grant reversal (R58) is also a correction, but it takes
  credit out.
- **R22** [✅] Opening a second account for a programme the
  member already has an account for is allowed and is the normal way
  to give someone more credit (R20). The new account simply waits its
  turn (R27).
- **R23** [X] The validity window must have its end after its
  start. A window entirely in the past is rejected at open time.
- **R24** [✅] Opening an account records the admin's stated
  reason on its opening ledger entry (R79).

## Which account pays

- **R25** [✅] When credit is needed for a programme, usable
  accounts bound to that programme are consumed first.
  Only when none remains is a general account touched.
- **R26** [X] A usable event-bound account is never
  bypassed in favour of a general one, whatever their relative ages or
  balances.
- **R27** [✅] Within each kind, accounts are consumed
  oldest-first by opening time. A newer account is untouched until
  every older one of the same kind is empty or expired.
- **R28** [✅] An account that is expired is skipped in the
  selection order exactly as an empty one is, and the search continues
  to the next candidate.
- **R29** [✅] Where the occurrence cost exceeds any single
  usable account's balance, the cost is split across accounts,
  following the same order (R25–R27), until it is met. A split is
  recorded as one ledger entry per account touched.
- **R30** [✅] The whole cost is taken or none of it is. If the
  usable accounts together cannot meet it, nothing is deducted and the
  operation is refused (R41).
- **R31** [✅] Concurrent deductions against the same account
  are serialised, so two simultaneous attendance writes can never take
  the same last credit twice.

## The occurrence cost

- **R32** [✅] The cost of one occurrence is a property of the
  programme, obtained through a single lookup on the event.
- **R33** [✅] That lookup returns 1 for every programme at
  present.
  The value is not stored on the event and the events table is not
  modified by this subsystem.
- **R34** [✅] Every rule in this document that spends,
  refunds, or checks credit is expressed in terms of the cost returned
  by R32, never a hard-coded 1. Moving the value into the events table
  later must require no change beyond the lookup itself.

## Enrollment gating

- **R35** [✅] Requesting to join, having a join request approved,
  **accepting an invitation**, being assigned and being trial-assigned
  each require the member to hold at least 1 usable credit for that
  programme, chosen by the selection order (R25–R28). → 422
  `INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT`.
- **R35a** [✅ #446] **Being invited does not.** An invitation is an offer,
  not a commitment: nothing is spent, and the member may never take it
  up. Gating it stops an admin inviting someone they intend to fund next,
  which is an ordinary way for a season to start — invitations go out
  before payments come in.

  The block sits at **accept**, where the member actually joins, and at
  assign and trial-assign, which put someone into a programme without
  their consent. So an unfunded invitation can be extended and simply
  cannot be acted on until it is funded.
- **R36** [✅] The threshold is 1 credit, not the cost of the
  whole programme. A member with a single credit may join; they will
  be stopped at the first occurrence they cannot pay for (R41).
- **R37** [✅] Usability is judged against **the current
  instant** at the
  moment of the enrollment action, not against the programme's start
  date. An account that expires before the programme begins still
  admits the member; they are simply blocked from attending once it
  lapses (R41).
- **R38** [X] An enrollment action failing the credit check
  is refused outright. Nothing is created, no partial state is left, and
  no notification is sent.
- **R38a** [✅] The refusal names the member who lacks credit and
  the amount required; it does not tell the caller to contact an admin.
  Two of the gated paths — assign and trial-assign (R35) — *are* driven
  by an admin, so directing them to themselves says nothing. What the
  caller needs is which member cannot be funded.
- **R39** [✅] The enrollment check does not deduct anything.
  Credit is spent by attendance (R44), never by joining.
- **R40** [X] Credit is not re-checked when a programme is
  split. A split changes the timetable and moves no enrollment
  (programme R24d), so the members stay enrolled and their bound
  accounts keep paying (R7).

## Spending on attendance

- **R41** [X] Marking attendance for a member with
  insufficient usable credit is refused. The member remains enrolled
  and is not removed.
- **R41b1** [X] **No role bypasses the credit check**, super-admin
  included. `attendance_requirements.md` R11 enumerates the temporal
  guards a super-admin may override; this is not one of them. A mark that
  cannot be paid for is not an audit correction — it is an unfunded
  charge, and creating it would put the ledger out of step with
  attendance, which R44a exists to prevent.
- **R41a** [X] The credit check runs before the attendance
  record is written, within the same transaction. A refused mark
  leaves no attendance record, no charge, and no partial state.
- **R41b** [✅] Where attendance is marked for several members
  in one action, each member is settled independently. Members with
  sufficient credit are marked; members without are refused
  individually, and the caller is told which were refused and why. One
  member's lapsed credit never prevents another member's attendance
  from being recorded.
- **R41c** [✅] A member refused under R41b is not an error
  condition for the action as a whole. The action succeeds, reporting
  what it did and what it declined to do.
- **R42** [✅] There is no stored "disabled" or "suspended"
  state on the enrollment. Being blocked is simply the absence of
  usable credit, computed at the moment of the write.
- **R43** [✅] Opening a new account for a blocked member
  restores them immediately. No admin step re-enables anything,
  because nothing was disabled.
- **R44** [✅] Marking `present`, `absent`, or `late` deducts
  the occurrence cost (R32) once for that
  `(member, event, occurrence)` triple.
- **R44a** [✅] Charging is expressed as a reconciliation, not
  as a set of per-transition actions. An occurrence is charged if and
  only if the member's resulting attendance status is `present`,
  `absent`, or `late`; every other resulting state — the two leave
  statuses, and the absence of a record — is uncharged. Every
  attendance transition that decides a status — marking, clearing,
  approving or rejecting leave, cancelling the occurrence —
  re-reconciles the charge to that rule. Requesting leave does not: a
  charge already taken stands until the request is decided.
- **R44b** [✅] Reconciliation is idempotent. Reconciling an
  occurrence that already matches its required state moves no credit,
  so a repeated or retried attendance write cannot charge twice.
- **R45** [✅] Approved leave costs nothing.
- **R46** [✅] Moving between `present`, `absent`, and `late`
  costs nothing further — the credit was already taken and stays
  taken. A second deduction for the same occurrence is impossible.
- **R47** [✅] Moving a charged record to approved leave
  refunds the cost. Marking a chargeable status over approved leave
  charges it. Rejecting a leave request restores the status the record
  had before the request (or no record), and charges only if that
  status is chargeable. Clearing the record refunds. Each of these
  follows from R44a rather than being implemented as its own rule.
- **R48** [✅] Cancelling an occurrence for which attendance
  was already recorded is treated, for credit purposes, exactly as
  approved leave: the cost is refunded. A cancelled occurrence is
  uncharged whatever its attendance records say, so this is a clause of
  the charging rule (R44a) rather than a branch in the cancellation path —
  cancelling reconciles every marked member, like every other transition,
  and only then deletes the records (attendance R21b). Un-cancelling
  therefore has nothing to re-charge against: the restored occurrence is
  unmarked and is charged as the register is marked again (#337).
- **R48b** [✅] For camps the window is ordinary: attendance opens 30
  minutes before the start and a camp occurrence carries no cancellation
  lead time, so a register marked early and then cancelled happens
  without anyone doing anything unusual. Credit does not apply to camps
  (R6), so that path never reaches these rules: a camp register is
  marked whatever credit its members hold.
- **R48c** [✅] For programmes and one-offs the two windows are
  deliberately disjoint — an occurrence cannot be cancelled from 30
  minutes before its start, which is the instant its register opens
  (programme R17, one-off R5). The case is therefore reachable only by a
  **super-admin cancelling an occurrence retrospectively**: the occurrence
  was called off in the room, nobody told the server, and a register was
  marked that should not exist.

  That override is the reason these refund rules exist rather than being
  dead text, and the event documents state it. What happens to the
  attendance marks themselves on such a cancellation is #337, not this
  document; the credit consequence is settled here — the charge is
  refunded.
- **R49** [✅] A refund always returns to the exact account
  that paid, in the exact amounts taken, including when the cost was
  split across several accounts (R29).

## Trials

- **R50** [✅] A trial is funded by an event-bound account
  marked as a trial account. Trials are not free.
- **R51** [✅] Trial credit is spent by attendance under the
  same rules as ordinary credit (R44–R47).
- **R52** [✅ #447] A member whose trial credit is exhausted is
  **removed** from the programme rather than blocked. This is the only
  case where running out of credit ends an enrollment: a completed trial
  is over, not paused waiting for a top-up.

  The removal is a departure like any other: it records when the member
  left, so an ended trial covers no occurrence after it (attendance
  eligibility and the member's own history both go by that date). Its
  reason is the fixed code `trialCreditExhausted`, which tells an ended
  trial apart from an admin removing a member who happened to be on
  trial. The member is told that their trial ended, with its start and
  end, and the attendance response that ended it says so, so the coach
  does not learn it from the member vanishing on the next refresh.

  **Correcting the mark that ended a trial** does not restore it. If that
  mark is changed to leave or cleared, the credit is refunded (R47), but
  the member stays removed: the trial is over. The refunded credit sits in
  the trial account, bound to the programme, until an admin acts (R72).
  The admin may assign the member to a trial again, which that credit now
  funds.
- **R52a** [✅ #447] This removal is a **system action**, not an admin one.
  It takes no credit disposition, and needs none: the balance that
  triggered it is by definition zero, so R71 has nothing to resolve.
- **R52b** [✅ #447] It must still write an audit row, with the system as
  the actor and the exhausted trial as the reason, and the coach whose
  mark triggered it named alongside, so the removal still has a person
  behind it (R82, R84).
- **R53** [✅] A trial account is never selected to pay for a
  non-trial enrollment, and an ordinary account is never selected to
  extend a trial. Exhaustion means exhaustion (R52) even where the
  member holds other usable accounts. A trial account is an event-bound
  account carrying a trial flag, not a separate kind (R2).

## Corrections and refunds

- **R54** [✅] An admin reverting a wrongly-marked attendance
  returns the credit to the account it came from (R49).
- **R55** [✅] A refund reaches its source account even if
  that account's validity window has since closed. The credit is
  restored where it belongs; whether it can then be spent is a
  separate question (R61).
- **R56** [✅] A refund reaches its source account even if that
  account has since been emptied and its remainder transferred away
  under R66–R70. The transfer does not redirect later refunds.

  Note (#479): a transfer closes its source account, so the refund
  **reopens** it — the closure is cleared and the reopening is audited
  with the refund as its reason — and the refunded credit can then be
  spent, extended or transferred again like any open balance.
- **R57** [✅] A refunded expired account may then be dealt
  with by an admin under the expiry rules (R61–R64) — extended, or
  transferred with a penalty.
- **R58** [✅] An admin who opened an account in error — wrong
  member, wrong amount, wrong programme — reverses the grant, in whole or
  in part, with a stated reason. The reversal is a new ledger entry, not
  an edit.
- **R59** [X] A grant cannot be reversed beyond what remains
  unspent. Where credits have already been consumed, the admin must
  address the attendance records first (R54).
- **R60** [X] Every correction carries a mandatory reason.
  There is no unexplained movement of credit in either direction.

## Expiry

- **R61** [✅] Expired credits are never destroyed, forfeited
  automatically, or swept. They remain in the account, unspendable,
  until an admin acts.
- **R62** [✅] An admin may extend an account's validity
  window, with a stated reason. An extension only moves the end
  forwards. This is the only way an expired account becomes usable
  again.
- **R63** [✅] An admin may instead transfer an expired
  balance to a new general account, applying a penalty (R66). This
  closes the expired account.
- **R64** [✅] Admin and member views both show an expired
  balance and describe it as expired. It is never hidden from the
  member who owns it.

## Leaving a programme

- **R65** [✅] A member who drops out voluntarily — a withdrawal
  request the admin approves (R73) — has their remaining event-bound
  balance moved to a **new general account** opened for the purpose.
- **R65a** [✅] A transfer never targets an existing account
  and offers no choice of destination. Since accounts are never topped
  up (R20), the only lawful destination is a new one, created by the
  transfer itself. The source account is closed by the same operation.
- **R66** [✅] The transfer applies a penalty: a flat number of
  credits subtracted from the balance before it moves. There is no
  ratio, multiplication, or rounding involved.
- **R66a** [✅] A penalty is one figure for the departure, not
  one per account. Where a member holds more than one account bound to
  the programme it is taken from them oldest-first until used up.
  Holding two packages instead of one never doubles the penalty.
- **R67** [✅] A penalty of zero is a valid and expected case.
- **R68** [✅] Where balance minus penalty is greater than
  zero, a new general account is opened holding the remainder. Where
  it is zero or less, no account is opened and the whole balance is
  forfeited to the penalty.
- **R69** [✅] A penalty is bounded by the balance of the
  account it is applied to. A penalty larger than that balance is not
  an error; it takes the balance to zero and no further. It never
  produces a negative account (R12), and the shortfall is not carried
  anywhere. The member's **general** accounts are not consulted, not
  reduced, and not even examined.
- **R69a** R69 and R66a do not conflict. A departure penalty is a single
  flat figure taken across the member's **bound** accounts oldest-first
  until it is used up (R66a); charging it once per account would punish a
  member for having bought two packages instead of one. R69 bounds it at
  the bound balance in total and stops it reaching anything else.
- **R70** [✅] Removal by an admin follows the same mechanics
  (R65–R69). The difference is one of intent, not of rule: the penalty
  may be set to consume the entire balance.
- **R71** [X] A member cannot be removed from a programme
  while credit sits unresolved in an account bound to it → 422
  `CREDIT_DISPOSITION_REQUIRED`. The admin supplies the disposition —
  the penalty, the new account's validity window, and a reason — as part
  of the removal. A member holding no bound credit needs none.
- **R72** [✅] Nothing is converted automatically. An
  event-bound balance left behind by any path other than removal sits
  in place until an admin acts (R61). The one exception is a terminated
  programme's release (R73c).
- **R72a** [✅] A departure resolves every balance the member
  holds on the programme, expired ones included. Expiry never destroys
  credit (R61), so a lapsed balance is precisely the kind a removal
  would otherwise strand.
- **R73** [✅] Approving a withdrawal request is the admin's
  decision and carries the same disposition (R71). Rejecting one
  leaves every account untouched.
- **R74** [✅] A member with a pending withdrawal request is
  still enrolled and is still charged for occurrences they are marked
  against, until the withdrawal is approved.
- **R73a** [✅] **Terminating a programme releases every balance bound
  to it**, at the **cutoff** rather than at the moment of termination.
  Each member's unspent bound credit moves to a general account keeping
  its validity window, with no penalty and no disposition asked for: the
  club is ending the programme, so no member is at fault
  (programme R10, R10a; lifecycle L20). Each member is notified.
- **R73b** [✅] Releasing at the cutoff rather than at the call is the
  same reasoning as R74a. A terminated programme keeps running and keeps
  charging until its cutoff, so how much is actually left is unknown
  until the last occurrence has passed. Nothing is reserved in the meantime.
- **R73c** [✅] This is the exception R72 must name. R72 says nothing
  converts automatically outside a removal; termination is the second
  case, and it is automatic precisely because there is no decision to
  take. R75's fresh admin-set validity window does not apply either — a
  release keeps the window it had, since the member is not being asked to
  accept new terms for credit they already hold.
- **R73d** [✅] A scheduled sweep performs the release, and the same
  sweep performs R74a's deferred settlements. It must be idempotent
  (lifecycle L20a). Each deferred settlement is applied on its own, so
  one that fails is left pending for the next sweep and the rest still
  settle.
- **R73e** [✅] A bound account whose window has already closed
  at the cutoff is **not** released. Moving it into a general account
  keeping a window that has closed would buy nothing, so it stays bound,
  unspendable, for an admin to extend or transfer (R61–R63).
- **R74a** [✅] A departure that leaves covered occurrences behind it
  settles at the last of them, not at the instant of the call. A member
  who leaves while an occurrence they are covered for is under way
  stays chargeable for it (R74). The disposition is stated at the
  departure and applied when the last covered occurrence has passed; the
  balance stays bound and spends normally until then. Nothing is
  reserved — how much such a member will be charged is unknowable in
  advance, since leave, a cancelled occurrence and never being marked all
  cost nothing. Where no covered occurrence remains, settlement is
  immediate, which is the common case. See
  [`programme_requirements.md`](programme_requirements.md) R29c–R29f.

  A deferred settlement is applied in the name of the admin who stated
  it: its ledger entries and the general account it opens name them
  (R82), not the sweep that ran it. Its validity window was checked when
  it was stated, and is applied as stated even if it has closed by the
  time the sweep runs (#485).

  **Left open by design: a late mark after settlement.** A session before
  the departure may be marked *after* the settlement has moved the bound
  balance out, inside the attendance edit window (attendance R13). The
  member was on that session, so they are still covered for it and it
  charges by the normal selection order (R25): from their general credit,
  which includes the account the transfer created. They pay exactly once,
  and nothing is refunded or re-settled. It is rare and corrects itself,
  so the settlement is not held open for it.
- **R75** [✅] The new general account created by a transfer
  carries its own fresh validity window, set by the admin at the time.
  It does not inherit the closed account's window.

## Movement between members

- **R76** [X] Credit never moves between members. There is
  no transfer, gift, or reassignment operation from one member's
  account to another's: a transfer's new account always belongs to the
  member whose account it drained.
- **R77** [✅] An admin wanting the same effect removes credit
  from one member's account with a reason and opens an account for the
  other with a reason. The two operations are independent and no
  relationship between them is recorded.
- **R78** [X] There is no guardian, payer, or sponsor concept.
  Credit belongs to the member whose account holds it (R1); no other
  member can read it.

## Ledger and audit

- **R79** [✅] Every movement of credit — opening, spending,
  refunding, reversing, penalising, transferring — is a ledger entry.
  A validity extension is recorded too, at an amount of zero.
- **R80** [X] Ledger entries are never modified and never
  deleted. A correction is a new entry that offsets an earlier one.
- **R81** [✅] Every entry records its amount, its account, the
  acting user, the time, its reason, and — where it arises from
  attendance — the occurrence that caused it.
- **R82** [✅ #448] An entry arising from an automatic deduction or
  refund records the staff member whose action triggered it — the
  coach or admin who recorded the attendance — not a system
  placeholder. Credit always has a person behind it. A deferred
  departure settlement (R74a) names the admin who stated the
  disposition. The one movement with no person behind it is the release
  of a terminated programme (R73a): the club ended it, and no member is
  at fault.
- **R83** [X] There is at most **one charge row** per
  `(member, event, occurrence)` triple, enforced at the storage layer by
  a unique constraint rather than in application logic only.
- **R82a** [X] A charge is keyed by `(member, programme,
  occurrence)`, and the programme's id never changes (R7), so a split
  cannot move an attendance row away from its charge: re-marking an
  occurrence after a split finds the charge it already has and does not
  charge a second time.
- **R83a** The rule is about the row, not about the number of movements.
  A refund marks that row refunded; a later move back to a chargeable
  status re-charges the same row (R47). So a triple may be charged,
  refunded and charged again over its life — what it may never have is
  two live charges at once.
- **R84** [✅] Credit operations write audit rows in addition
  to ledger entries, consistent with the rest of the server: actor,
  target member, action, and affected account id. This covers
  deductions and refunds as well as the account lifecycle — the ledger
  says what the balance is, and the audit log is where someone goes to
  ask what happened.
- **R85** [X] Failed operations (refused enrollment, refused
  attendance, rejected grant) write neither a ledger entry nor an
  audit row. Both record movements, not attempts.

## Deployment gating

The subsystem is optional. One club runs on credits and another does
not, from the same codebase, differentiated by deploy configuration —
the same way the two products already differ.

- **R92** [✅] Whether the credit system is active is fixed by
  deployment configuration, not by an admin setting at runtime. It is
  not a system preference and there is no endpoint that changes it.
- **R93** [✅] The default is **disabled**. A deployment that
  says nothing about credit behaves exactly as it did before this
  subsystem existed.
- **R94** [✅] Where the system is disabled, every credit
  endpoint still exists and answers, refusing uniformly with a single
  documented "not available on this deployment" response (503
  `CREDIT_SYSTEM_DISABLED`). The API
  surface does not vary with configuration — only the answer does. This
  keeps one published schema across both products, and keeps the
  disabled case a single check at the edge rather than a condition
  threaded through every module.
- **R94a** [X] The refusal is uniform. No credit endpoint,
  when disabled, performs part of its work, consults a table, reads an
  account, or returns anything but the refusal — not even an
  authentication or permission check.
- **R95** [✅] Where the system is disabled, every integration
  point is inert: no enrollment is gated, no attendance is charged, no
  account is consulted, and no ledger entry is written. Enrollment and
  attendance behave precisely as they do today.
- **R96** [✅] Enabling or disabling destroys nothing. A
  deployment switched off keeps its accounts and its ledger intact, and
  switching it back on resumes from them.
- **R97** [✅] The response shape of an endpoint shared with
  other subsystems never depends on the flag. The per-member report on
  bulk attendance marking (R41b) is returned by every deployment; where
  credit is disabled, the refused list is simply always empty. Neither
  the API surface nor any response shape varies with configuration.
- **R98** [X] Where the system is disabled, a credit
  disposition supplied on removal or withdrawal (R71) is rejected
  (→ 422 `CREDIT_DISPOSITION_NOT_APPLICABLE`)
  rather than silently ignored. A client sending it has misunderstood
  the deployment.
- **R98a** [X] The same refusal answers a disposition supplied
  on a departure from a camp or a one-off, where credit never applies
  (R6), even on a deployment that runs on credits.
